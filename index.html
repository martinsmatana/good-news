what it is

good news retold in fabric

There is plenty of bad news everywhere - but many good things also happen every single day. They just get lost in the flood of negative headlines.

Year of Good News is a project that collects real positive stories from all over the world and retells them through textile art - sewn and put together from old clothes, scarves, and carpets.

The idea is simple. This project highlights these small stories and brings them into the light.

The project was created by Martin Smatana - an internationally recognized director of animated films.

why it was created

born from bad news

The pandemic was the trigger - but today it is no longer the main theme of the project.

The project was born out of bad news. In March 2020, when the pandemic started, Martin was looking for a way to stay calm and hopeful - something that would inspire him instead of bringing him down. So he purposefully started looking for good news from recent years.

His conclusion after two years of collecting: despite all the terrible things we read about every day, many beautiful things are also happening - we just easily overlook them. He decided to make these stories visible and tell them in his own way.

technique and method

stitched by hand

The material is not accidental - it gives the pictures warmth, softness and a human touch that naturally fits the theme of good news.

Week after week, Martin looked for positive news, saved them as pictures or cut them out of newspapers and magazines. He then hand-sewed each story using textiles - old clothes, scarves and carpets.

Martin had already used this technique in his animated films. Thanks to this, Year of Good News has an instantly recognizable visual style - soft, playful and full of detail.

What started as a hobby quickly became an internationally successful art project.

publications and fan base

books and press

  • Three published books, translated into several languages and available across four countries: United States, United Kingdom, Germany and South Korea.
  • The German edition (Ein Jahr voller guter Nachrichten, Pattloch Verlag) was at one point the third best-selling book in Germany.
  • Regularly posting new artworks on Instagram every week built a loyal community that looked forward to the next piece of good news.
  • Strong international media coverage - for example the Austrian magazine Woman, Bored Panda, Designboom and others.
  • Books available on Amazon (Martin Smatana author store).

a fan base that connects generations

The project did not have just one target group:

  • grandchildren buy the book for their grandparents to tell them at the end of the year: "Look grandma - this year wasn't actually so bad - all these beautiful things happened";
  • adults give it to other adults as a Christmas gift;
  • several schools chose good news as their theme of the year - they had good news bulletin boards in the hallways and made their own fabric illustrations inspired by the project.

Year of Good News is not just for kids or just for adults - it works across all ages.

Italy → UK

A boy who walked to grandma.

An 11-year-old boy moved from England to Palermo, Italy. He had not seen his grandmother from London for over a year during the lockdown. As all the flights to London were cancelled due to the pandemics, he decided to walk. His parents first told him "No!" for 50 times until his father finally agreed to go with him. They walked across Italy, Switzerland and France, they got lost a few times, got bloody feets, fought off wild dogs and slept under the stars. After 93 days, 2 800 kilometres and two weeks in quarantine, they made it to Trafalgar Square so Romeo could finally give a hug to his granny who couldn't believe her eyes.

why we are building on it now

an antidote to doomscrolling

How to teach children (and adults) to understand the world without being consumed by fear?

Until now, this was a completed project for Martin - the books were published, translated and lived their own life. Now we want to bring it back to life, but not by repeating the exact same format.

The original "why" (the pandemic) is gone; the new "why" is more relevant than ever.

The new main theme: doomscrolling, anxiety from negative news, media literacy and mental well-being.

Year of Good News stops being about the pandemic and becomes an antidote to a world that overwhelms us with bad news.

The biggest advantage compared to other projects: we are not starting from scratch. We are building on a finished body of work - three books, over 150 illustrations, a proven brand and a ready-made aesthetic. We have a world that people already know and love.

from paper into motion

We are building the project as a single Intellectual Property (IP) with multiple layers, where each medium adds something new instead of just repeating the same thing.

format · one

one episode is one piece of good news

Each episode will retell one real positive story.

format · two

a connecting element

Episodes will be linked by a uniform connecting element that unifies the whole series and sets it apart from a standard anthology. This element will visit and carry the stories without ever replacing their actual heroes.

format · three

one season is one year

Following the books - the season will go through the four seasons of the year, and at the end the viewer will look back and see that more good news gathered than fear.

format · four

short episodes

A few minutes of good news - meaningful and the exact opposite of endlessly scrolling through bad news. Form and content matching.

visual and animation concept

textile stop-motion

The series will literally bring the book illustrations to life - same technique that made the original illustrations, now set in motion.

  • Pure textile stop-motion animation - semi-plastic and textured.
  • Textile characters and backgrounds lay on a glass surface and are filmed from above using a vertically mounted DSLR camera.
  • Soft cinematic lighting and frame-by-frame animation.

A combination of the best of two worlds - the flat 2D layout where characters lie down, combined with the soft textile materials, real light and camera work of 3D puppet animation. The result is a unique artistic style.

education and community

an educational wrap

Doing systematically what schools were already doing on their own.

  • Workshops where children make their own textile good news.
  • Worksheets and methodologies for schools - media literacy, handling emotions, critical reading of news.
  • A traveling exhibition of the original artworks and the making-of the animation.
  • Screenings followed by discussions.

education lives in the wrapping not in the story

The series never lectures or moralizes - it tells stories and shows things. The knowledge and context are carried by the materials around it.

how we fund the project

two kinds of funding at once

Audiovisual production funds and education / culture funds. This combination is its core strength and makes the funding more resilient.

series production

  • Creative Europe MEDIA - development and production of the series. For Slovakia the Mini-Slate scheme is advantageous (designed for countries with lower production capacity, meaning less competition), or co-development and support for TV / online content.
  • National sources: The Slovak Audiovisual Fund (AVF) and the broadcaster (RTVS / partner broadcasters) - development, production, broadcasting and mandatory national co-funding.

transmedia and cultural layer

  • Creative Europe — Creative Innovation Lab (CROSS) for the digital / interactive layer as a cross-sector innovation.
  • Creative Europe — Culture for the traveling exhibition and cultural cooperation.

educational package

  • Erasmus+ (KA210 for an easy start, KA220 for larger cooperation) - methodologies, partnerships with schools and teacher training.
  • Domestic cultural sources (FPU) and foundations.
